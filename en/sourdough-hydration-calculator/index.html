❓ FAQ

What is dough hydration?

Dough hydration is the weight of water as a percentage of the total flour weight. 500 g flour + 350 g water = 350 ÷ 500 × 100% = 70% hydration. Higher hydration means a wetter dough, an airier crumb, and more skill needed to handle and shape it.

How do I count the flour and water inside my starter?

A 100% hydration starter (1:1) is half flour and half water by weight. 100 g of starter = 50 g flour + 50 g water. When computing total hydration you must add the starter's flour and water to the main dough — this calculator does it automatically.

What hydration is best for which bread?

60-65%: pizza and bagels, stiff and easy to shape. 70%: classic country loaves, the golden ratio. 75-80%: baguettes and ciabatta with open crumb. Above 80%: focaccia-style very wet doughs that need multiple stretch-and-folds.

How do I change the hydration of a recipe?

Add water to raise hydration, remove water to lower it. To go from 70% to 75% with 500 g flour, increase water from 350 g to 375 g. Change in steps of about 5% and watch how the dough behaves before adjusting again.
