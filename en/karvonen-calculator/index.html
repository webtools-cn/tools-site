Frequently Asked Questions

How is the Karvonen method different from "220 − age"?

The basic method estimates max HR then takes a flat percentage, ignoring individual resting heart rate. Karvonen uses heart rate reserve (HRR = MHR − RHR) as the base and adds resting HR back, reflecting true workload — most accurate for trained people with low resting HR.

What target heart rate zone is right for me?

General fitness uses 50%–85% of heart rate reserve: 50–60% fat-burn/recovery, 60–70% aerobic base, 70–80% endurance, 80–85% anaerobic/HIIT. Beginners should start at 50–60%.

How do I measure resting heart rate accurately?

On waking, before getting out of bed, place two fingers on your wrist or neck and count beats for 60 seconds. Average 2–3 days for reliability. Athletes often sit below 60 bpm.

Is "220 − age" for max HR always accurate?

It is a population average with ±10–12 bpm individual variation. Fine for training guidance; for precision use a graded exercise test. This tool is for fitness guidance only, not medical advice.
