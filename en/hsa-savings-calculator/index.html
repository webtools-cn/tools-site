📖 How to Use

Free online HSA Savings Calculator to estimate tax savings, compound growth and retirement planning for your Health Savings Account. The HSA is the only "triple tax-free" account in the US: contributions are tax-deductible, growth is tax-free, and withdrawals for qualified medical expenses are tax-free. Enter your contribution amount, tax rate, and expected return to see the long-term value of your HSA.

❓ FAQ

What is the triple tax advantage of an HSA?

① Tax-deductible contributions reduce your taxable income; ② Tax-free growth — investment earnings inside the HSA are not taxed; ③ Tax-free withdrawals for qualified medical expenses. The HSA is the only "triple tax-free" account in the US tax code.

What are the 2025 HSA contribution limits?

2025 IRS limits: Single $4,300, Family $8,550. An additional $1,000 catch-up contribution is allowed for those aged 55+. These limits typically adjust annually for inflation.

Can HSA funds be invested?

Yes. Most HSA providers allow investing in mutual funds/ETFs once your balance exceeds a threshold (typically $1,000-$2,000). The invested portion enjoys tax-free growth similar to a 401(k), and withdrawals for medical expenses remain tax-free.

What's the difference between HSA and FSA?

HSA balances roll over year to year, never expire, travel with you across jobs, and can be invested. FSAs are typically "use it or lose it" and cannot be invested. HSAs require a High-Deductible Health Plan (HDHP); FSAs have no such requirement.

How can I use my HSA in retirement?

After age 65: withdrawals for medical expenses remain tax-free; non-medical withdrawals are taxed as ordinary income (no 20% penalty). This makes the HSA an excellent supplemental retirement account — covering both medical and living expenses.
