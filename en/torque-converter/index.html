Frequently Asked Questions

What are common torque units?

Common torque units include Newton-meters (N·m), kilonewton-meters (kN·m), pound-force feet (lbf·ft), pound-force inches (lbf·in), kilogram-force meters (kgf·m), and dyne-centimeters (dyn·cm). Auto repair commonly uses N·m and lbf·ft. 1 lbf·ft ≈ 1.356 N·m.

How many Newton-meters in one pound-force foot?

1 pound-force foot (lbf·ft) ≈ 1.3558 Newton-meters (N·m). This is the most common torque conversion in automotive work. For example, tightening wheel nuts to 100 lbf·ft equals approximately 135.6 N·m.

How do I convert N·m to kgf·m?

1 kilogram-force meter (kgf·m) = 9.80665 Newton-meters (N·m). Conversely, 1 N·m ≈ 0.10197 kgf·m. Japanese auto repair manuals often use kgf·m for torque specifications.

What is the difference between lbf·ft and lbf·in?

Both are imperial torque units. 1 lbf·ft = 12 lbf·in. lbf·ft is used for high-torque applications like engine bolts, while lbf·in gives finer resolution for small fasteners like interior trim screws. This tool supports conversion between both.
