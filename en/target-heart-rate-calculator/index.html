Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Karvonen (heart-rate-reserve) method?

The Karvonen method treats heart-rate reserve (HRR = max HR − resting HR) as the truly trainable range, so target HR = resting HR + HRR × intensity%. Compared with using a flat percentage of max HR, it accounts for your personal resting heart rate and gives a safer, more individualized result. Max HR is estimated as 220 − age.

What is the difference between target HR and max HR?

Max HR (MHR) is the theoretical upper limit of your heart in all-out effort; target HR is the zone you should hold during a given workout. For example, a 30-year-old has MHR ≈ 190 bpm, but the fat-burn zone (60-70%) target is about 142-155 bpm — not sprinting to 190.

What are the five training zones for?

Zone 1 (50-60%) recovery; Zone 2 (60-70%) fat burn; Zone 3 (70-80%) aerobic endurance; Zone 4 (80-90%) anaerobic/threshold; Zone 5 (90-100%) max effort. Pick the zone that matches your goal — consistent Zone 2 work is best for fat loss and base endurance.

Is this formula accurate for everyone?

220 − age is a population average with an individual spread of about ±10-12 bpm. Athletes or people with cardiovascular conditions should use a treadmill test for personalization. This tool is for reference only and is not a substitute for medical advice.
