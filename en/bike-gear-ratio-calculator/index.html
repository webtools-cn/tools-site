Frequently asked questions

What is gear ratio?

Gear ratio = chainring teeth ÷ cog teeth. A higher ratio is "harder" (faster but more effort); a lower ratio is "easier" (better for climbing). It is the most direct measure of drivetrain gearing.

What are gear inches?

Gear inches = ratio × wheel diameter (inches), a traditional unit for gearing feel. About 50 gear-inches is moderate; higher favors speed, lower favors climbs.

What is development?

Development = ratio × wheel circumference, the meters the bike travels per full crank revolution. It is the international standard and is more intuitive (the unit is simply meters).

How do I estimate speed from cadence?

Speed (km/h) = development (m) × cadence (rpm) × 60 ÷ 1000. For example, development 6.9 m at 90 rpm gives about 37.3 km/h.
