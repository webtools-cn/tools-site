FAQ

What is Kelvin?
Kelvin (K) is the base unit of temperature in the International System of Units (SI), named after British physicist William Thomson (Lord Kelvin). 0K (absolute zero) is the theoretical minimum temperature, equal to -273.15°C.

When to use Kelvin?
Kelvin is widely used in scientific research, physics and engineering, especially in thermodynamic calculations. In daily life, most countries use Celsius, while the United States uses Fahrenheit.
