🧮 How It Works

Drip system design aims for even emitter flow and keeps total flow within the mainline and pump capacity:

Emitters per line = ⌈ length × 12 / emitter spacing ⌉ (imperial) Total emitters = emitters per line × number of lines System flow rate = total emitters × flow per emitter Daily water usage = system flow rate × run hours per day Suggested zones = ⌈ system flow / 12 GPM ⌉ (3/4-inch mainline limit)

❓ FAQ

How much water does a 1 GPH emitter deliver per day?
A 1 GPH emitter delivers 1 US gallon (about 3.78 L) per hour. Run 30 minutes a day and it delivers about 0.5 gallons (1.9 L); run 1 hour and it delivers about 1 gallon. Vegetable beds typically run 20-40 minutes per day, adjusted for soil and crop needs.
How do I choose an emitter flow rate? Sandy vs clay soil?
Sandy soil drains fast and spreads water poorly sideways, so use 2 GPH (8 L/h) fast emitters with tighter spacing. Clay soil drains slowly and spreads well, so 0.5-1 GPH (2-4 L/h) slow emitters work better and avoid waterlogging. For standard vegetable beds, use 1 GPH (4 L/h) emitters at 12-inch spacing.
How many emitters can one mainline supply?
A 3/4-inch PE mainline is safely rated around 12 GPM (about 45 L/min). Above 12 GPM, split the system into zones or upgrade to a 1-inch mainline. This calculator suggests the number of zones you need so you can plan valves and rotation.
Do I really need a filter on a drip system?
Yes. Drip emitter paths are very narrow (0.6-1.2 mm) and clog easily. For well or river water, install a 120-mesh disc or screen filter. Even on city water, use at least a 60-80 mesh filter and add an end flush valve to rinse the lines regularly.
