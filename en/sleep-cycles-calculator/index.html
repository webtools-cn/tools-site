Sleep Cycle Basics

What is a Sleep Cycle?

A sleep cycle is the pattern your brain follows as it cycles through different sleep stages. One complete cycle lasts about 90 minutes and includes three stages: Light Sleep (NREM 1-2), Deep Sleep (NREM 3, also called slow-wave sleep), and REM Sleep (dreaming stage). Adults typically experience 4-6 complete sleep cycles each night.

Why 90 Minutes Matters

Research shows that waking up naturally at the end of a sleep cycle (during light sleep or REM) leaves you feeling refreshed and alert. Being jolted awake by an alarm during deep sleep can cause sleep inertia — grogginess, headaches, and mental fog lasting minutes to hours. Planning your total sleep time in 90-minute multiples significantly improves wake-up quality.

Sleep Stages Explained

  • Light Sleep (~50%): Heart rate slows, body temperature drops, easily woken. This is the crucial transition from wakefulness to deeper sleep.
  • Deep Sleep (~20%): Body repair, tissue growth, and immune system strengthening occur here. Waking during deep sleep feels extremely disorienting.
  • REM Sleep (~25%): Brain is highly active, eyes move rapidly, most dreaming happens. Critical for memory consolidation and learning.

How to Use

Two Calculation Modes: This tool offers both 'Wake-up time calculation' and 'Bedtime calculation' modes. Choose 'What time should I wake up?' and enter when you plan to go to bed — the tool calculates optimal wake-up times. Account for Falling Asleep: Important! Calculations assume you fall asleep instantly. In reality, most people need 10-20 minutes to fall asleep. We recommend going to bed 15-20 minutes earlier than the calculated time to allow for natural sleep onset.

Choose Recommended Times: Times marked with ⭐ Recommended correspond to 5-6 complete sleep cycles (7.5-9 hours), the optimal range for most adults. If you feel refreshed after 4 cycles (6 hours), you may need less sleep than average.

Use Cases

Workday Planning: If you need to wake up at 7:00 AM, use this tool to find optimal bedtimes around 9:30 PM (6 cycles) or 11:00 PM (5 cycles). Choose what works for you and maintain a consistent schedule for better weekday energy levels.

Early Flights / Exams: When you need to wake up unusually early (for an early flight or exam), back-calculate the best bedtime to avoid being woken from deep sleep. For example, if you need to wake at 4:00 AM, going to bed at 10:00 PM (4 cycles) may be the best option.

Jet Lag Adjustment: When traveling across time zones, use this tool to plan sleep according to your destination time zone and gradually adjust your body clock. Combine with light exposure (morning sunlight, evening blue-light avoidance) for best results.

FAQ

What is a sleep cycle?

A sleep cycle is a pattern where your brain cycles through different sleep stages. One complete sleep cycle lasts about 90 minutes, including light sleep, deep sleep, and REM (rapid eye movement) sleep. Adults typically experience 4-6 full sleep cycles each night.

Why is the 90-minute sleep cycle important?

Waking up at the end of a sleep cycle (during light sleep or REM) leaves you feeling refreshed and alert. Being woken during deep sleep causes sleep inertia — grogginess, headaches, and mental fog lasting minutes to hours. Planning total sleep time in 90-minute multiples significantly improves wake-up quality.

How many sleep cycles do I need?

Adults are recommended 5-6 sleep cycles per night (about 7.5-9 hours). However, individual needs vary — some people do fine with 4 cycles (6 hours), while others need 6 cycles (9 hours). The key is to adjust based on how you feel and find your optimal number.

How can I improve my sleep quality?

Tips to improve sleep quality: 1) Maintain a consistent sleep schedule with the same bedtime and wake time every day; 2) Avoid screens 1 hour before bed (blue light suppresses melatonin); 3) Keep your bedroom cool, quiet, and dark; 4) Avoid caffeine and heavy meals before bedtime; 5) Exercise daily but avoid intense workouts within 3 hours of sleep.
