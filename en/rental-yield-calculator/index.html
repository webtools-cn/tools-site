What is Rental Yield?

Rental yield is a core metric for measuring the return on a property investment, comparing annual rental income to the property price to assess its value. Whether buying a home, apartment or commercial property, rental yield helps investors quickly judge whether a property is worth investing in.

Gross rental yield only considers rental income, while net rental yield deducts all operating costs such as property tax, insurance, maintenance and management fees, giving a more realistic picture of actual returns. When comparing different properties, net yield is the more reliable reference.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is rental yield?

Rental yield is an important metric for measuring property investment returns, calculated as annual rental income divided by property price, expressed as a percentage. It helps investors quickly assess the rental return level of a property.

What is the difference between gross yield and net yield?

Gross yield only considers the ratio of rental income to property price, without deducting any costs. Net yield subtracts all operating costs such as property tax, insurance, maintenance and management fees from annual rent, giving a more realistic picture of actual return.

What is a good rental yield?

Generally, a gross yield of 3%-5% is normal in major cities, while 5%-8% is considered good in secondary cities. Net yield is usually 1-2 percentage points lower. A net yield above 8% is typically a quality investment.

How can I improve rental yield?

You can improve rental yield by: 1) Lowering purchase costs and finding below-market properties; 2) Increasing rent through renovations; 3) Reducing vacancy rates by choosing high-demand areas; 4) Controlling operating costs through efficient maintenance and management.
