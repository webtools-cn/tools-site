Formula

Blended C:N = total carbon mass ÷ total nitrogen mass, where:

Total C = Σ( weight_i × C_i% / 100 )
Total N = Σ( weight_i × N_i% / 100 )
Blended C:N = Total C ÷ Total N

Because C:N is a ratio, the weight unit (kg or lb) does not affect the result — it only changes the displayed total mass.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the carbon-to-nitrogen ratio (C:N)?

The C:N ratio is the mass ratio of carbon to nitrogen in compost feedstock. Carbon fuels microbial energy ("browns"), while nitrogen builds microbial protein ("greens"). Too high slows decomposition; too low causes ammonia odor and nitrogen loss.

Why is 25:1 – 30:1 the ideal range?

Research and practice show that a C:N between 25 and 30 gives the highest microbial activity, fastest curing, and best nitrogen retention. A common mistake is adding too many browns (leaves, cardboard), pushing the ratio too high.

My ratio is too high (carbon-heavy) — what do I do?

Add more "greens": kitchen scraps, grass clippings, coffee grounds, or manure, which are nitrogen-rich. A small amount of urea (~46% N) can also rebalance the mix.

What if the ratio is too low (nitrogen-heavy)?

Excess nitrogen releases smelly ammonia and attracts pests while losing nitrogen. Add browns — dry leaves, straw, wood chips, or shredded cardboard — to raise the ratio.
