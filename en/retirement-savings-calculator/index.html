FAQ

What compound interest formula does this calculator use?

We use the monthly compound model: FV = P × (1+r)^n + M × [(1+r)^n - 1] / r. Where P = current principal, M = monthly deposit, r = monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12), n = total months. This assumes end-of-month deposits — the standard financial formula.

What's different about retirement vs. regular savings?

Retirement savings benefit from long-term compound growth. At 7% annual return, starting at age 25 with $500/month yields about $920,000 by age 65. Starting at 40 yields only $285,000. Start early!

Where should I invest my retirement savings?

Strategy varies by age: 20s-30s — stock-heavy (7-10% expected return); 40s-50s — balanced (5-7%); 55+ — conservative (3-5%). This tool is for estimation only; consult a financial advisor.

Why account for inflation?

Inflation erodes purchasing power. $1 million in 30 years at 2.5% inflation is worth about $470,000 in today's dollars. We recommend entering 2-3% inflation to see real purchasing power.

How much do I need for FIRE?

The FIRE movement follows the 4% rule: retirement savings should be ≥ 25× annual expenses. If you spend $40,000/year, you need $1,000,000 saved. Use this calculator to see if your savings plan gets you there.
