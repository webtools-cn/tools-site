Calculation logic:
1. Stitches across = width(cm) ÷ 10 × stitches per 10 cm; rows down = height(cm) ÷ 10 × rows per 10 cm.
2. Yarn per stitch ≈ 2.5 × (stitch width + stitch height), where stitch width = 10 ÷ stitches per 10 cm, stitch height = 10 ÷ rows per 10 cm (cm).
3. Total length = total stitches × yarn per stitch × (1 + waste%); weight = length(m) × 100 ÷ (m/100g).

❓ FAQ

How do I measure my gauge?

Knit a 10×10 cm swatch (stockinette) with your chosen yarn and count the horizontal stitches and vertical rows. That gives your stitches/rows per 10 cm — the most reliable input, better than guessing.

Is "yarn per stitch ≈ 2.5×(stitch width + stitch height)" accurate?

This is a widely used industry estimate that accounts for the loop path length. Actual yarn used varies with stitch type (stockinette, brioche, etc.), tension and fiber, usually within ±10%. Add the waste margin to be safe.

Should I buy an extra skein?

Yes — keep at least 5%–10% margin, especially for colorwork or large projects. This tool has an adjustable waste percentage; raise it as needed.

How are inches handled for gauge?

Select "per 4 inch" and the tool converts to per-10 cm using 4 inch ≈ 10.16 cm, working seamlessly with cm dimensions.
