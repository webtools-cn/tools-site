📖 How to Use

Measuring Your Pool

Use a tape measure to measure the inside dimensions of your pool (not including wall thickness). For irregular shapes, measure the longest and widest points and use the kidney shape option for an estimate.

Key measurements:
• Length: longest inside wall distance
• Width: widest inside wall distance
• Depth: water surface to pool floor (not total wall height)
• Sloped pools: measure shallow end and deep end separately

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate the volume of my pool?
For a rectangular pool: length × width × average depth × 7.48 = gallons. For a circular pool: π × radius² × depth × 7.48. For an oval pool: π × (length÷2) × (width÷2) × depth × 7.48. For a kidney-shaped pool: length × width × depth × 0.75 × 7.48. Average depth = (shallow end + deep end) ÷ 2. One cubic foot = 7.48 gallons.
How many gallons is my pool?
It depends on your pool shape and size. A typical backyard rectangular pool (15×30 ft, 4.5 ft average depth) holds about 15,100 gallons. A small round above-ground pool (15 ft diameter, 4 ft deep) holds about 5,300 gallons. Use this calculator with your pool dimensions for an accurate result.
How much does a gallon of pool water weigh?
One gallon of water weighs about 8.34 pounds (3.785 kg). One liter weighs about 1 kg. A 15,000-gallon pool weighs approximately 62.6 tons. Knowing the water weight helps assess ground load and structural safety.
How often should I run my pool pump?
It is recommended to filter your entire pool volume 1-2 times per day. Turnover time = pool volume ÷ pump flow rate. For example, a 15,000-gallon pool with a 60 GPM pump takes about 4.2 hours per cycle. During heavy summer use, aim for 2 cycles per day.
How much does it cost to fill a pool?
It depends on your pool volume and local water rates. For a 15,000-gallon pool at the U.S. average rate of about $4 per 1,000 gallons, the fill cost would be about $60. Use this calculator with your local water rate for a specific estimate.
