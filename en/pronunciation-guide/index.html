FAQ

What types of phonetic symbols are there?

The main system is IPA (International Phonetic Alphabet). This tool shows both British (RP - Received Pronunciation) and American (GA - General American) IPA phonetics for easy comparison.

How can I use phonetics to pronounce words accurately?

1) Learn the pronunciation rules for each symbol (e.g. /θ/ is a dental sound); 2) Pay attention to stress marks (ˈ indicates primary stress); 3) Listen to standard pronunciations and imitate; 4) Use this tool's audio playback to practice repeatedly. The key is practice — just reading phonetics is not enough.

Why does the same word have different pronunciations?

Main reasons: 1) British vs American differences (e.g. 'bath' UK /bɑːθ/ vs US /bæθ/); 2) Part of speech changes stress (e.g. 'record' noun /ˈrekɔːd/ vs verb /rɪˈkɔːd/); 3) Regional dialect variations; 4) Connected speech changes (linking, weak forms).

What is the difference between phonetics and phonics?

Phonics teaches children to guess pronunciation from letter patterns — rule-based but with many exceptions (e.g. 'tough'/'through'/'though' look similar but sound different). Phonetics is a precise symbol system where one symbol always represents one sound. Learning phonetics is the foundation for mastering English pronunciation.
