Formula

Distance to fret n from nut = scale × (1 − 2^(−n/12)) Adjacent gap = position(n) − position(n−1) Remaining scale = scale − position(n) = scale × 2^(−n/12) where 2^(1/12) ≈ 1.059463 (equal-tempered semitone ratio)

FAQ

How is fret spacing calculated?

Using equal temperament: each semitone raises pitch by a factor of 2^(1/12), so the vibrating string length shrinks proportionally. Distance to fret n from the nut = scale length × (1 − 2^(−n/12)). This holds for any instrument fretted in semitones.

Why is the 12th fret exactly at half the scale?

The 12th fret is the octave (frequency doubles). Since 2^(−12/12)=0.5, position = scale × (1 − 0.5) = half the scale. It is the key check that your calculation is correct.

What is the scale length?

The vibrating string length from the nut to the bridge (before compensation). A typical electric guitar is about 25.5″ (648 mm), classical about 25.6″ (650 mm), bass about 34″ (864 mm).

How do I handle real bridge compensation?

Real strings need the bridge moved slightly back (compensation) for accurate octaves. This tool gives the ideal geometric positions; fine-tune the saddle after measuring at the 12th fret.
