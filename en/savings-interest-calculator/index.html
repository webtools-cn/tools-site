📖 How to Use

This savings interest calculator helps you quickly calculate deposit interest earnings with both simple and compound interest modes:

Step 1: Choose Calculation Mode

Click "Simple Interest" or "Compound Interest" to switch. Simple interest is calculated only on the principal (common for CDs). Compound interest adds interest to principal for subsequent periods (common for savings accounts and investments).

Step 2: Enter Principal

Enter your deposit amount, e.g., $10,000. Any amount is supported.

Step 3: Set Interest Rate

Enter the annual interest rate as a percentage. High-yield savings accounts currently offer 4-5% APY, while traditional banks offer 0.01-0.5%.

Step 4: Set Term

Enter the deposit term in years. Supports decimals (e.g., 0.5 = 6 months).

Step 5: View Results

Click "Calculate" to instantly see total amount, interest earned, and effective annual yield.

💡 Simple vs Compound Interest

Example: $10,000 principal at 4.5% for 5 years:

  • Simple: Interest = $10,000 × 4.5% × 5 = $2,250. Total = $12,250
  • Compound (Annual): Total = $10,000 × (1+4.5%)⁵ = $12,461.82. Interest = $2,461.82
  • Compound (Monthly): Total = $10,000 × (1+4.5%/12)^60 = $12,519.61. Interest = $2,519.61

The longer the term and higher the rate, the more compound interest outperforms simple interest. Higher compounding frequency (monthly, daily) yields slightly more.

❓ FAQ

What's the difference between simple and compound interest?

Simple interest is calculated only on the principal. Compound interest is calculated on the principal plus accumulated interest (interest on interest). For example: $10,000 at 5% for 3 years — Simple: $1,500 interest; Compound: $1,576.25 interest. The gap widens dramatically over longer periods.

Are bank savings accounts simple or compound interest?

Most savings accounts use compound interest, typically compounded daily or monthly. Fixed-term deposits (CDs) may use simple interest. Our calculator supports both modes so you can match your actual account type.

How can I maximize my savings interest?

1) Choose high-yield savings accounts (HYSA) offering 4-5% APY; 2) Opt for compound interest with higher frequency (daily/monthly); 3) Keep money deposited longer — avoid early withdrawals; 4) Consider CD laddering for better rates. Use this calculator to compare scenarios.

Is savings interest taxable?

In the US, savings account interest is taxable as ordinary income. You'll receive a 1099-INT form from your bank if interest exceeds $10 per year. This calculator shows pre-tax interest — consult a tax professional for your specific situation.
