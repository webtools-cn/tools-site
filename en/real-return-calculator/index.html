💡 Why Real Return Matters

Nominal returns can be deceiving — your account balance may grow, but if inflation is higher, your purchasing power could actually shrink. Real return tells you what truly happened to your money.

Fisher Equation: (1 + Nominal) = (1 + Real) × (1 + Inflation)
Approximation: Real ≈ Nominal − Inflation

❓ FAQ

What if nominal return is below inflation?

Real return is negative — your purchasing power is declining. This is why keeping all your money in low-yield savings accounts can be a losing strategy long-term.

What's the long-term real return of stocks?

US stocks historically returned ~10% nominal, ~3% inflation, ~7% real. Results vary significantly by country and time period. Past performance doesn't guarantee future results.
