FAQ

What is equilibrium curing?

Equilibrium curing adds salt and cure at a fixed percentage of meat weight; the salt diffuses evenly through the chill period and never oversalts — far more controllable than traditional salting, the standard method for home charcuterie.

What is the difference between Prague #1 and #2?

#1 contains sodium nitrite and is for meat that is heated or cured short-term (bacon, jerky, brine). #2 adds sodium nitrate for long dry-aged salami. This calculator targets #1. Never eat pink salt directly — always dilute by formula.

What is nitrite ppm and is 156 ppm safe?

ppm means mg of nitrite per kg of meat. 156 ppm ≈ 156 mg/kg, within the US FDA common limit of ≤200 ppm for cured meat — it inhibits botulism and fixes the pink color.

Can I skip the pink salt?

Yes, but you lose preservation and the pink color and risk botulism. Only advised for a "quick cure" cooked and eaten within 24 hours — not for long curing or drying.
