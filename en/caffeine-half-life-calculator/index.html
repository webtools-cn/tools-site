📖 How to Use

The Caffeine Half-Life Calculator helps you understand how caffeine metabolizes in your body, so you can optimize your coffee schedule and protect your sleep quality.

Step 1: Select or Enter Intake

Click a common drink button above to quickly set caffeine content, or enter an exact amount manually. A standard brewed coffee contains ~95mg, a large latte ~200mg, and a Red Bull ~80mg.

Step 2: Set Half-Life

Caffeine half-life varies significantly between individuals. Non-smoking healthy adults: 3-5 hours. Smokers metabolize faster (~3 hours). Pregnant women, those with liver disease, or people taking certain medications may metabolize slower (up to 10+ hours).

Step 3: Read the Decay Curve

The chart shows your caffeine decay curve with key markers: current level, projected bedtime level, and complete clearance time. If bedtime caffeine exceeds 50mg, consider moving your last coffee earlier.

🎯 Health Tips

Sleep Quality: Research shows caffeine consumed within 6 hours of bedtime significantly reduces sleep quality. Caffeine-sensitive individuals should stop by 2 PM. Residual caffeine above 50mg can impair deep sleep.

Optimal Timing: The best time for coffee is 1-2 hours after waking (around 9:30-11:30 AM), when cortisol levels naturally start declining. Caffeine's alertness effect peaks during this window without disrupting natural hormone rhythms.

Daily Limit: The FDA recommends healthy adults consume no more than 400mg of caffeine daily (about 4 cups of coffee). Excessive intake can cause palpitations, anxiety, insomnia, and digestive issues.

Special Populations: Pregnant women should limit to 200mg/day. Adolescents and children should avoid caffeine. Antibiotics, oral contraceptives, and antidepressants can significantly slow caffeine metabolism.

💡 Background

What Does Half-Life Mean?

The half-life is the time needed for caffeine concentration in your body to drop by half. If you consume 200mg with a 5-hour half-life, you'll have 100mg after 5 hours, 50mg after 10 hours, and 25mg after 15 hours. Caffeine decays exponentially, not linearly.

Factors Affecting Caffeine Metabolism

Genetics (CYP1A2 enzyme activity) is the biggest factor. Smoking accelerates metabolism (half-life drops to ~3 hours). Oral contraceptives and pregnancy slow it (half-life extends to 5-10 hours). Liver disease significantly slows metabolism. Grapefruit juice inhibits caffeine-metabolizing enzymes.

Caffeine Sensitivity Test

If you struggle to sleep after an afternoon coffee, you're likely caffeine-sensitive with a longer half-life. If you can drink coffee 3 hours before bed and sleep fine, you're likely a fast metabolizer. This tool helps you find your personal "caffeine cutoff time."

❓ FAQ

How long until caffeine is completely out of my system?

With an average 5-hour half-life, 200mg of caffeine takes about 25-30 hours to drop below 5mg (nearly undetectable). However, most people stop feeling effects after 12-24 hours. The physiological effects of caffeine typically last shorter than complete metabolic clearance.

Why do people metabolize caffeine so differently?

It's primarily determined by the CYP1A2 gene in your liver. About 50% of people are "fast metabolizers" (half-life ~3-4 hours), 40% are "moderate" (~5-6 hours), and 10% are "slow metabolizers" (up to 8-10 hours). Slow metabolizers face higher heart disease risk from coffee and should limit intake.

How accurate is this calculator?

It's based on the pharmacological exponential decay model: remaining = initial × (1/2)^(time/half-life), validated by extensive clinical research. However, individual metabolism varies with genetics, health, and medications. Results are estimates for reference.

How long before bed should I stop drinking coffee?

General advice: stop at least 6 hours before bedtime. If you're a slow metabolizer (half-life 7+ hours), allow 8-10 hours. Use this calculator with your half-life and bedtime to find your precise "caffeine cutoff time."
