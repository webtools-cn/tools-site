FAQ

What is chaptalization and why add sugar?

Chaptalization is adding sucrose or dextrose to grape must before fermentation to raise the final alcohol content. Under-ripe grapes have low natural sugar, producing thin, low-alcohol wine; adding sugar compensates. Note: added sugar only raises alcohol — it does not add fruit flavor or sweetness, since yeast converts it fully to alcohol.

How much sugar per liter raises ABV by 1%?

Rule of thumb: about 17 g/L sugar raises ABV by 1%. This calculator multiplies (target ABV − current potential ABV) by 17 to get sugar per liter, then multiplies by volume for the total.

How do I measure current sugar? Brix vs specific gravity?

A refractometer gives Brix (sugar % by weight); a hydrometer gives Specific Gravity. They convert: Brix ≈ (SG − 1) × 250. If you have a lab sugar concentration (g/L), pick "g/L" for best accuracy.

Is there a limit to how much sugar I can add?

Typical wine yeast tolerates about 14–18% ABV. Above 18% most yeast stall, leaving residual sugar. Some regions legally restrict chaptalization. The tool warns when the target is high — always follow your yeast specs.
