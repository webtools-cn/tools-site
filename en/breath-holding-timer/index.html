📖 How to Use

The Breath Holding Timer is a free online tool to help you test and improve your apnea ability. It's very easy to use:

Step 1: Prepare with Deep Breaths

Before clicking start, take 3-5 deep breaths to fully oxygenate your body. Relax your mind and body. Sit comfortably or lie down in a safe environment.

Step 2: Start Holding

After your final deep inhale, click the "Start Holding" button. The timer begins immediately. Keep holding your breath — do not breathe.

Step 3: Stop Timer

When you feel the urge to breathe, click "Stop". The system displays your breath-hold time, rating, and history records.

Safety Warning

Never practice extreme breath-holding in water or without supervision. If you feel dizzy or unwell, stop immediately. This tool is for recreational reference only and does not replace professional medical advice.

💡 Tips to Improve Breath-Hold

  • Progress Gradually: Practice 1-2 times daily, try to hold a few seconds longer each time
  • Stay Relaxed: Tension consumes more oxygen — staying calm extends your breath-hold time
  • Prepare Well: Take 3-5 deep breaths before holding (hyperoxic breathing), but avoid hyperventilation
  • Practice on Empty Stomach: Wait at least 1-2 hours after eating — a full stomach restricts diaphragm movement
  • Room Temperature: Avoid extreme temperatures; room temperature is optimal for breath-holding

❓ FAQ

How long can an average person hold their breath?

The average person can hold their breath for 30 seconds to 1 minute. Trained swimmers and freedivers can hold for 2-5 minutes, with the world record exceeding 11 minutes. Your time will improve with consistent practice.

Is breath-holding harmful?

Moderate breath-holding practice is safe for healthy individuals and may even benefit lung capacity and respiratory control. However, people with cardiovascular disease, hypertension, or who are pregnant should avoid this exercise or consult a doctor first.

Will my data be lost?

All data (including best records and history) is stored in your browser's local storage. Your records will not be lost unless you clear browser data. No data is ever uploaded to any server.

Is extreme breath-holding safe?

We strongly advise against attempting extreme breath-holding (over 4 minutes) alone. Oxygen deprivation can cause loss of consciousness, which is especially dangerous in water. Always practice in a safe environment with another person present.
